Why a goose

How geese fly together

Geese cross whole continents twice a year, and they do it as a team. The V they fly in is a working arrangement, and it maps neatly onto how good project teams work.

  • They share the lift

    Each goose flies a little behind and to the side of the one ahead, where the air spilling off its wingtip rises. In the V, every bird spends less energy than it would flying alone.

    In Goose: one shared plan. The Timeline shows every project and deadline, so nobody pulls against the rest of the team.

  • They take turns at the front

    Leading is the hardest position because there is no rising air to ride. Birds in a V swap places, so the work at the front is shared across the flock.

    In Goose: every project has an owner and every team a lead, and both are easy to hand over.

  • They keep calling

    Geese call to each other constantly in flight, which is thought to help the flock stay together and keep its spacing.

    In Goose: project notebooks for meetings and decisions, and a weekly summary you can copy and send.

  • They learn the route together

    Young geese migrate with their parents and learn the way by flying it, stopover by stopover.

    In Goose: notes, decisions and tasks stay with the project, so someone joining halfway sees the whole route so far.

Timeline

The whole year on one screen

Tasks

List, board or calendar

The same tasks in the view that suits the moment.

  • List grouped by due date (overdue, today, next 7 days) or by project
  • Board: drag between To do, In progress and Done
  • Calendar: drag a task to another day to move it

Projects and notebooks

Notes that turn into tasks

Every project has a notebook for meetings, notes and decisions. Any line can become a task, linked back to where it came from.

  • Project wall with status, progress, open and overdue tasks and recent activity
  • Decisions collected on the side, so nobody asks "what did we agree?"
  • The project's own list, board and calendar, one click away

Why Goose

Built for teams whose work is part project, part ongoing

Goose started as a tool for technical consulting, where a client study with a hard deadline runs next to work that never really ends. Generic task apps handle one or the other.

Deadlines and ongoing work side by side

Fixed-term projects end; training, publications and client care do not. Goose treats both properly, so the ongoing work stays visible instead of hiding in a backlog.

Strategy you can check

Goals are linked to real projects and tasks, so their progress updates as the work gets done. You can see which goal is falling behind before the quarterly meeting.

Meeting notes that don't get lost

Write the notes where the project lives. Turn any line into a task in one click, and keep decisions where the next person will find them.

Clients can look in

Invite a client as a Guest and they see their project, read-only, and nothing else in your workspace.

Calm to use

A short menu, clear words, and a Create or Save button in every form, so you always know when something is saved.

Small and mid-sized teams that run projects for clients or for themselves.

Security and privacy

Each person sees only what they are part of

Client work is confidential, so Goose decides who sees what on the server, before anything reaches a browser.

Rules enforced by the database

Visibility is checked by the database itself for every row of data. A person's browser never receives a project or task they are not allowed to see.

Data kept in the EU

Your workspace is stored in a data centre in Ireland.

Sign in with an email link

No password to remember or leak. Goose sends a one-time link to your inbox.

Tested on every change

Every update runs automatic checks that sign in as an admin, a member, a guest and an outsider and confirm what each one can see and change.

Separate workspaces

Each workspace is sealed off from every other. You can belong to several, with a different access level in each.

You choose who joins

Only admins invite people and set their access level. Removing someone hands their projects back to the admin.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Can I use Goose on my own?

Yes. Everyone who signs up gets their own workspace, where they are the admin and can plan goals, projects and tasks alone. Invite people whenever you are ready.

Can I be part of more than one workspace?

Yes. Your own workspace and every workspace you are invited to sit under one sign-in, and you switch between them. Your access level can differ in each.

Can my clients see their project?

Invite them as a Guest. They see the projects they are a member of, read-only, and nothing else.

What is the difference between a fixed-term and an ongoing project?

A fixed-term project has an end date, like a client study. An ongoing project keeps going, like training or publications. Both can support goals, and the Timeline draws them differently so you can tell them apart.

How is goal progress calculated?

From what is linked to the goal: the tasks of its projects, tasks linked to it directly, and its sub-goals. If nothing is linked yet, you set the progress yourself.

Where is my data stored?

In the European Union, in a data centre in Ireland. Who can see what is enforced by the database, not by the page in your browser.

Why is it called Goose?

Geese travel thousands of kilometres by flying together: they share the effort, take turns leading and keep in touch the whole way. That is the kind of teamwork Goose is built for.
